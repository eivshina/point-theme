Meet Katya

Katya Ivshina is a Boston-based content creator, AI educator, and speaker dedicated to making artificial intelligence accessible. Her journey began when she moved to the United States alone at 17 to pursue a degree in Mathematics at Princeton University. With a talent for breaking down complex AI concepts into relatable, actionable insights, Katya has become a trusted voice in AI education. Her authenticity has earned her a community of over 100,000 followers and more than 30 million views across social media platforms.

Katya’s speaking and outreach work includes keynote addresses and talks at major events such as the Girls in Engineering and Robotics Symposium, HacKnight at BB&N High School, ENYGMMa at CUNY, and the Northeastern Graduate Women in Science and Engineering workshop. She has collaborated with leading AI and education-focused companies including Unriddle, Juni Learning, and Jenni AI.

Previously, Katya interned as a machine learning engineer at Apple with the Advanced Computation Group, where she presented her work to company leadership. She has conducted research across machine learning, geometry, topology, and neuroscience. Her papers cover topics such as persistent cup-length, knot Floer homology, and exoplanets, including a first-author paper in the *Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series*. Her work has earned her honors including the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship, Harvard’s GSAS Prize Fellowship, the D.E. Shaw Zenith Fellowship, Princeton’s Alberto Santos-Dumont Prize for Innovation, and the Manfred Pyka Memorial Prize in Physics. She is regularly invited to present her research at major academic conferences, such as Computational Geometry Week in Japan, the Young Topologists Meeting in Stockholm, and the London Geometry and Machine Learning Summer School.

In addition to her digital work, Katya is passionate about community organizing and educational outreach. She co-founded and led the "02.24.2022" student organization at Princeton in response to the war in Ukraine, organizing a solidarity letter signed by over 550 affiliates, educational initiatives, and fundraising events. She co-directed a five-week academic support and mentorship program pairing 50 Ukrainian refugee students with mentors from Princeton, Harvard, and MIT.

As a first-generation immigrant and a woman in STEM, Katya shares her experiences, challenges, and achievements to inspire others to pursue their goals with confidence. She is also a dedicated dancer and is currently completing a PhD in Applied Mathematics at Harvard University.
